Groww, first-time mutual fund investors

Why first-year SIPs stop, and a passbook built to keep them going

Losses make new investors think about stopping. Failed payments and unanswered "where is my money" questions are what make them actually stop: payment lapses appear in 35% of stop stories, disappointing returns in 21%.

I built SIP Passbook, a concept that tracks every instalment like an online order, from bank debit to units allotted. Its AI explainer passed 18 of 20 hand-labelled test questions with no critical errors.

Google Photos, finding old photos

Finding the photo you remember but can't describe

People rarely have the words a search box needs. Across 720 retrieval stories, they most often remembered what was in the photo, roughly when, and who was there. When memory failed, the missing detail was most often the date.

I built The One Where, an MVP that treats finding a photo as a conversation: it narrows to a few photos for you to recognise, and says when it can't tell two apart instead of guessing.

Myntra, wishlist to purchase

Why saved items don't get bought

Shoppers save items they like but aren't sure about, then leave Myntra to settle the doubt: comparing prices, checking the item is genuine, asking friends. Value for money was the top friction (257 of 516 high-confidence stories), ahead of fit and size (120).

I built Myntra Match Check, a prototype that answers "is this right for me?" on a saved item, so the decision can happen inside the app instead of across five tabs.

Case studies

How I read a business from the outside: what to measure, what went wrong and what I'd do next. Built from public data and filings, and for ChatGPT, my own survey and interviews.

  • Rapido

    Metrics and dashboard

    Rapido earns two different ways (commission on bikes, subscriptions on autos and cabs), so one GMV number misreads the business.

    The call: North Star is weekly fulfilled rides, held honest by contribution per fulfilled ride. When growth doesn't pay, rebalance supply by zone rather than raise the fee.

  • Dunzo

    Post-mortem

    Quick commerce was winnable; Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart all scaled. Dunzo specifically lost.

    The cause: it left the errands category it created to fight a grocery war as the smallest player, funded by one investor with veto rights, so when the bet failed it could neither raise nor sell.

  • CRED

    Product teardown

    A once-a-month product (credit card bills) trying to become a daily super app.

    The call: chasing daily use erodes exclusivity, CRED's only lasting moat. Win on timing and value per transaction instead: a calmer home screen, value-first onboarding and a premium UPI play.

  • Strava

    Monetisation strategy

    180M users, yet an estimated 2–5% pay, and most free users never even reach the paywall.

    The call: it's a timing and value-communication failure, not price. Fix the India price, show people their own data first, and offer premium at milestone moments.

  • ChatGPT voice input

    Adoption study

    89% of students surveyed send WhatsApp voice notes daily, yet only 11% use ChatGPT's voice input weekly. Primary research with a 38-student survey and 6 interviews.

    The fix: unbreak the mic entry point, add a preview before sending, and a Student Study Mode that makes voice feel like a study tool.
